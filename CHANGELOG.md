# Research releases

## 0.6.0 — 26 October 2026

Adds a candidate sharp zero-table cost theorem: for the full prefix of budgets, classical NEGEXP attains the unrestricted adaptive optimum to leading order as the threshold tends to zero and log(1/delta)*N/(P*m*tau) tends to zero, with delta at most 1/4. A finite hypergeometric bound and geometric envelope-value bins give the matching lower bound uniformly in growing target families. The design, envelope coefficient, and generic lower-bound ingredients are attributed to prior work; priority remains unestablished.

Adds 10,400 exact small-block inverse comparisons, four larger comparisons, and 56 conservative analytical cost certificates. The 93,438-byte source-pinned artifact reproduces byte for byte. All 182 tests pass, including eleven focused standard-library tests. Review corrected decimal-display rounding with a failing-then-passing regression. Symbolic population counts are never expanded into item tables; CLI behavior and caps are unchanged.

Records why a fixed-reference extension and a nonzero-instance impossibility direction were set aside as established ideas. The sharp theorem does not establish practical optimality at the earlier threshold 0.1, nonzero-table efficiency, model evidence, or independent human validation. Earlier source-pinned results are preserved.

## 0.5.0 — 26 October 2026

Extends the finite zero-table cost approximation to every declared budget grid containing 1, with factor 320 against the unrestricted adaptive optimum under the same threshold and confidence restrictions. The original factor 160 remains available on grids containing every power of four. The proof uses only declared witness targets and handles gaps between their disjoint blocks. A counterexample establishes failure of a bound uniform over all grids and confidence levels at fixed threshold when the baseline restriction is removed. It does not show failure at fixed confidence or for every grid without 1.

Adds a source-pinned exact checker and 122,900 cost comparisons, covering every containing-1 grid through pool size 12 plus five larger cases. A second run reproduces the saved artifact byte for byte. All 171 tests pass, including a regression that rejects huge scientific exponents before rational conversion. The zero-only workflow accepts the extended domain; four earlier design records and two actual v0.4.1 report replays are unchanged.

This is a theorem-scope extension using established auditing and geometric grouping techniques. Scientific priority, independent human validation, and real-model usefulness remain unestablished. Earlier source-pinned results are preserved.

## 0.4.1 — 26 October 2026

Adds a complete standard-library zero-only annotation workflow for the v0.4 whole-family decision. Planning uses exact rational inclusion probabilities, fresh OS random bits, unbiased integer rejection, a recorded tape, and an external commitment. Reporting verifies immutable content and exact annotations before returning pending, not certified, or certified. A positive value as small as `1e-400` cannot silently become zero; incomplete annotations cannot certify. Existing outputs are never overwritten.

The numerical proof shows that the conservative rational implementation retains the theorem's factor-160 expected zero-table cost guarantee. That guarantee remains conditional on independent uniform bits, correct fixed labels, and the original theorem restrictions. A recorded tape cannot establish its own randomness or prove that no other plan was tried.

All 159 tests pass, including 44 zero-cost/zero-workflow checks with site-packages disabled. End-to-end hand-authored arithmetic examples exercise pending, zero, and positive-label outcomes; report replay is byte-identical. Review added regressions for aggregate input limits and selection-independent output preflight, preventing resource rejection from filtering random selections. Earlier saved research evidence and source pins remain unchanged. This is a usability release, not a new model study or an external novelty determination.

## 0.4.0 — 26 October 2026

Adds a candidate finite-table cost approximation theorem for the established multi-target NEGEXP audit. For the declared structured budget grids, thresholds at most 1/8, and error probabilities at most 1/4, its expected distinct-label cost at the all-zero table is at most 160 times the unrestricted adaptive optimum. The proof includes finite census saturation and an explicit obstruction at thresholds approaching one.

Adds exact finite checks and rational geometry-specific cost brackets. The proposed contribution is the rank-family comparison, not a new generic auditing algorithm. The theorem does not bound costs on nonzero tables or establish optimality of the existing confidence-sequence engine. Earlier measured sources, results, and the v0.3 paper are preserved; the new follow-up has separate reproduction commands and source pins.

For the published 2,048-item geometry, the rational certificates bound expected NEGEXP cost within 1.398 times the adaptive optimum on the zero table. All 131 tests pass, including guards against disabled assertions, altered theorem metadata, and a protocol snapshot race. Finite arithmetic checks support the implementation; the universal result depends on the written proof, and its priority remains unestablished.

## 0.3.0 — 26 October 2026

Adds a sharp uniform finite-without-replacement envelope remainder, a second-order joint-limit profile, and a reproducible exact-arithmetic check. Corrects the initial abstract-only comparison with Fitas v2: the continuous normalizer and envelope design were already prior work.

Adds attributed variance-optimal and fixed-width proposals with numerical primal–dual brackets. The [follow-up comparison](docs/design-report.md) reuses the five verified v0.2 baselines and adds two methods on identical synthetic paths. It shows that the optimized proposals outperform the envelope on some stopping targets, and retains counterexamples. This is not a new holdout or a claim that either objective optimizes all unique-label costs.

Preserves measured v0.1/v0.2 sources and outputs. New runner refuses nonempty output directories, pins the base manifest, and checks reuse and source stability.

## 0.2.0 — 26 October 2026

Adds audits with distinct queries and confidence sequences valid at every labeled prefix. The statistical construction adapts established weighted-without-replacement betting methods, with explicit attribution and a finite-pool proof. The query plan freezes the random order, target budgets, proposal, bets, and confidence level before labels are supplied.

The new paired synthetic study compares five acquisition proposals on eight loss patterns at equal unique-label cost. It includes 4,000 method paths, 16,000 checkpoint records, raw measurements, a separate protocol and manifest, and an aggregate verifier. It measures the directional upper-test certificate; the CLI defaults to a more conservative interval rule. Advantages and counterexamples appear in the [report](docs/anytime-report.md).

The original fixed-draw commands and v0.1 measurements retain their semantics. No real-model evidence, external novelty determination, or human peer review is claimed.
