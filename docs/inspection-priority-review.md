# Inspection-game priority review

Bounded literature screen, 26 October 2026. This note records verified overlap and unresolved retrieval gaps; it does **not** establish scientific priority. No papers were downloaded, and no authors were contacted.

The comparison target is the [sharp zero-table cost theorem](sharp-cost-theorem.md): expected distinct-label cost, validity for every fixed loss table, almost-sure finite certification at zero, and an optimum allowing adaptive queries, correlated subsets, and empty-query lotteries. The v0.6 scope is the full-prefix best-of-n family in a joint rare-threshold, small-item-mass limit. The [structural follow-up](sharp-grid-structure.md) extends the ratio-one conclusion to arbitrary nonempty best-of-n budget grids. Neither formulation claims optimality for arbitrary normalized decreasing target rows; monotonicity and small atoms alone are insufficient. This literature note does not independently validate that extension.

## Verified overlap

**Avenhaus, Canty, Kilgour, von Stengel, and Zamir (1996), _Inspection games in arms control_, European Journal of Operational Research 90(3), 383–394.** Indexed primary full-text passages on printed p. 388 specify fixed inspection effort, stratified sampling with replacement, continuous sample-size allocations, and one weighted total-falsification constraint. They give a saddle-point solution and proportional allocation for small falsification. This is a direct older precedent for rare-falsification inspection allocation. The same page explicitly points to optimal **without-replacement** IAEA sampling in Avenhaus–Canty (1989), so a blanket distinction that older inspection theory uses replacement sampling would be wrong. [Author copy](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/~zamir/documents/Avenhaus_1996_European-Journal-of-Operational-Research.pdf); [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0377-2217(95)00261-8).

**Avenhaus and Canty (2007), _Formal Models for NPT Safeguards_, JNMM 35(4), 69–76.** The readable primary full text, printed p. 71, describes choosing each stratum's sample size to attain a specified nondetection probability when all falsification lies there. It guarantees that probability when falsification is distributed across strata and calls the allocation optimal under prior announcement, citing their 1989 paper and 1996 book. The passage does not define the comparator sufficiently to establish unrestricted expected-cost optimality. In particular, the permitted random sample sizes and cross-stratum dependence remain unclear. [Journal full text](https://resources.inmm.org/system/files/jnmm/vol_35/V-35_4.pdf).

**Krieger and Burr (2016), _Comparison of an Exact to an Approximate Sample Size Calculation for Attribute Testing_, JNMM 44(3), 45–48.** The readable primary full text compares exact hypergeometric inversion with the approximate IAEA formula for one homogeneous stratum, explicitly excluding substantial variation in item masses. Precise finite-population zero-detection calculations are therefore established ingredients, not a contribution here. [Journal full text](https://resources.inmm.org/system/files/jnmm/vol_44/V-44_3.pdf).

**Checkoway, _Methods of Post-election Confidence-level Auditing_, §4.10, p. 8.** Its near-optimality statement for NEGEXP is expressly restricted to independent sampling; the adjacent discussion permits improvement through correlated methods, including Saltman and PPEBWR. It does not supply the unrestricted comparison sought here. [Primary report](https://checkoway.net/papers/re/re.pdf).

## Unresolved priority gap

The proof-level comparison still requires **Avenhaus–Canty (1989), _Re-examination of the IAEA Formula for Stratified Attribute Sampling_, Proceedings of the 11th ESARDA Symposium, 351–356**, and **_Compliance Quantified_ (1996)**. The publisher provides summaries for [Chapter 2, Attributes Sampling, pp. 13–38](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511526510.004), and [Chapter 9, Inspector Leadership, pp. 207–244](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511526510.011), but the full chapters require access. The 1989 original was not retrieved. The [Handbook author draft](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/~zamir/papers/inspections.pdf) was inspected through indexed passages; its §3.3 result concerns noisy variable sampling and Neyman–Chuprov allocation. Direct retrieval of the complete Handbook and EJOR author PDFs failed.

No inspected passage establishes the joint growing-family theorem against the stated unrestricted comparator. That is a bounded finding, not novelty clearance. The defensible candidate contribution remains the explicit uniform cost lower bound and matching limit for the specified rank family, rather than NEGEXP, proportional inspection, hypergeometric inversion, or general unequal-probability auditing.
