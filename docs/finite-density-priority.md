# Prior-art comparison for the finite-density extension

Bounded primary-source comparison, 27 October 2026. The target is the [finite-density theorem](finite-density-theorem.md), with the same fixed-table, whole-family certificate and expected distinct-query objective as v0.8. Write `H=tau*log(1/delta)/epsilon^2` and `rho=N/(Pm)`. The proposed extension replaces the sufficient per-rank replication condition by `rho <= epsilon^2/[4*tau*log(4/delta)]`, giving the lower bound `ZH/16384`. It also adds the deterministic prefix upper bound `P*k_epsilon` and counterexamples to two proposed census-saturation formulas. This comparison distinguishes established ingredients from the complete statement; it establishes neither scientific priority nor practical savings. Primary full texts were read through web representations, without downloading PDFs.

The [frozen v0.8 comparison](nonzero-minimax-priority.md) remains applicable. [Fitas, arXiv:2608.21496v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21496v2), Theorem 2 and Supplement S7.2, supplies geometric score bands and adaptive stopped-transcript information bounds for a population estimation problem. [Sarkar and Baichoo, arXiv:2609.40190v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.40190v1), Appendix C.3, already combines a rare baseline, an order-epsilon perturbation within a band, per-label KL of order `epsilon^2/tau`, and summation over disjoint bands. Those arguments remain close precedents. The extension does not claim a new rare-risk KL principle, geometric grouping method, or generic sequential audit procedure.

The fixed-table argument still needs more than a bound on the stochastic prior mean. Its common safe prior is conditioned on **every** target risk lying at least epsilon below tau. The crossing bound and prefix-mixture identity control that event without a family union bound. Conditioning destroys product independence, so the proof computes transcript KL under the original product priors and caps each band's queries to bound the contribution of exceptional tables. Alternative-prior tables that fail to be unsafe also contribute an explicit error term. This bridges stochastic prior calculations to conditional-on-table safety and safe-class expected cost. Neither the concentration nor the mixture identity alone supplies that bridge, and the inspected sources do not by themselves establish the entire resulting minimax statement.

The prefix upper bound uses fixed losses in `[0,1]`: query the first `k_epsilon` ranks in every prompt, assign loss one to all unseen cells, and certify only when that worst-completion risk is safe. Here `k_epsilon` is the smallest k with `binom(m-k,min K)/binom(m,min K) <= epsilon`; a relevant-support census fallback ensures completeness on all safe tables. The counterexamples show why finite-population saturation needs the actual weight geometry. With `P=1,K={m}`, one cell suffices although the archive has m cells. With `P=1,K={m/2}`, even m, and sufficiently large m, a prefix of at most `ceil(log2(1/epsilon))` cells suffices on the margin class despite a positive support of `m/2+1` cells. Thus neither total archive size nor positive-support size repairs a universal `min(census,ZH)` lower formula. These are mathematical constructions, not measured audit performance or a claim of a new general weighted-query principle.

The density hypothesis is sufficient, not necessary, and the proof leaves the regime below it uncharacterized. The cited unweighted query theorem does not supply a sharp near-census deficit; binary exact-count constructions also retain integer constraints even though the actual loss class permits fractions. The bounded screen therefore supports a narrow description: a proposed fixed-grid theorem assembled from established concentration, monotone geometry, adaptive information arguments, and an explicit treatment of exceptional prior mass. It establishes no new concentration inequality or monotone-mixture result and does not establish novelty of the combination. Independent human proof review and broader priority assessment remain absent.
