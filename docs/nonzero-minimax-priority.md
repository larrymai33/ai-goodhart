# Prior-art comparison for the nonzero minimax extension

Bounded primary-source comparison, 26 October 2026. The linked full texts were read through their web representations; no PDFs were downloaded. This note does not establish scientific priority or survey the literature exhaustively.

The comparison target is the [nonzero finite-table theorem](nonzero-minimax-theorem.md): one globally valid auditor, a declared finite best-of-n budget grid, fixed fractional losses, and worst-case expected **distinct** query cost over tables with every risk at most `tau-epsilon`. The margin epsilon is supplied to the design. Its proposed matching order is `Z*tau*log(1/delta)/epsilon^2`, where Z is the exact finite-grid envelope normalizer. The lower bound requires the stated per-rank replication condition `P>=log(1/delta)/epsilon^2`; small item masses alone do not replace it.

## Closest best-of-n results

**Ricardo Fitas, _The geometry of AI validation: From structural blindness to reusable audits_, arXiv:2608.21496v2, 11 September 2026.** [Versioned primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21496v2). Theorem 2 gives a two-resource minimax law for worst-coordinate mean squared error with adaptive candidate acquisition and truth queries. Supplement S7.2 constructs disjoint geometric score bands, varies Bernoulli truth probabilities around one half, and applies stopped-transcript KL. Supplement S5 combines Bernstein bounds with a finite-family union bound and uses a geometric budget grid with total-variation interpolation. These are close precedents for the geometry and adaptive lower-bound architecture. The theorem's estimation objective and population candidate experiment differ from the fixed-table threshold decision studied here.

**Sohail (Neel) Sarkar and Shakuntala Baichoo, _Cheap to Draw, Expensive to Trust: Certifying Test-Time Scaling Curves_, arXiv:2609.40190v1, 30 September 2026.** [Versioned primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.40190v1). Appendix C.3, particularly “The multiscale term,” already uses a rare failure probability r, changes it by order epsilon inside one geometric band, bounds per-label KL by order `epsilon^2/r`, and sums the resulting requirements over disjoint bands. Its “Noisy labels” paragraph gives a related margin-sensitive lower bound at every law satisfying a noise floor. Thus the rare-variance, inverse-squared-margin, bandwise KL argument is established, not introduced here. Appendix B.5 controls a whole family through bracketing, and Section 7.6 gives a fixed-width design with a Bernstein family-confidence factor. The relevant cost and coverage statements concern the paper's answer-generation and correctness-query experiment; they do not state the present finite-table, arbitrary-grid global-decision result.

## Concentration and decision ingredients

**Stéphane Lhaut, Anne Sabourin, and Johan Segers, _Uniform concentration bounds for frequencies of rare events_, arXiv:2110.05826v2, 23 April 2022.** [Versioned primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.05826v2). Lemma 2.1 represents the empirical measure restricted to a rare region, conditional on its binomial sample count, as the conditional empirical measure multiplied by that count's fraction. The following paragraph combines conditional concentration with binomial Bernstein. Applying DKW to nested rank prefixes is a specialization of this established conditioning device.

**Henry W. J. Reeve, _A short proof of the Dvoretzky–Kiefer–Wolfowitz–Massart inequality_, arXiv:2403.16651v1, 25 March 2024.** [Versioned primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16651v1). Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 give localized, variance-sensitive empirical-CDF bounds. They provide another concentration precedent; the nonzero theorem does not claim a new DKW inequality. Layer-cake integration, or discrete summation by parts, is likewise an established way to transfer a CDF bound to bounded monotone weights.

The global certificate uses ordinary intersection–union testing. If any group contains an unsafe target, certifying all groups entails falsely certifying that particular group. Giving each group level delta therefore controls the single global error without dividing delta by the number of groups. This reasoning does not produce simultaneously selectable confidence intervals. The absence of a family-confidence logarithm must not be presented as an improvement over a theorem solving that stronger inference problem.

## What the inspected results do and do not imply

This bounded comparison did not identify a direct reduction from an inspected statement to the complete finite theorem. Their stated experiments and guarantees do not supply, together, the arbitrary-grid Z dependence, globally valid threshold decision, fixed-table distinct-label accounting, and the exact-count lower bound under the declared replication condition. Passing from stochastic response laws to a prior supported entirely on safe fixed tables requires an additional finite-population argument. Likewise, a high-probability estimation bound alone does not establish the claimed expected stopping cost.

These differences identify the work needed for a separate proof; they do not establish originality. Much of that proof combines established tools: geometric grouping, monotone likelihood ratios, rare-region conditioning, DKW, summation by parts, intersection–union testing, and adaptive change of measure. The candidate contribution is the particular finite-grid theorem and its matching parameter dependence. Claims of a first nonzero BoN audit, a new rare-risk lower-bound principle, or a generally optimal sequential auditing method would exceed this comparison.

The result also has a restricted practical interpretation. Epsilon is a design input, the lower bound needs many replicated prompts, and the upper constants are loose. Neither the theorem nor this literature comparison establishes useful finite savings on an existing benchmark.

## Why threshold-aware LP design was not pursued as the contribution

[Ronald L. Rivest's April 29, 2007 draft, _On Auditing Elections When Precincts Have Different Sizes_](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/Riv07a.pdf), already clips masses at a corruption threshold and studies independent-probability optimization through a knapsack adversary. [Michael J. Higgins, Ronald L. Rivest, and Philip B. Stark, _Sharper p-Values For Stratified Election Audits_ (2011)](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/HRS11.pdf), Section 4, equation (47), uses the continuous-knapsack relaxation inside an exponential audit certificate; Section 6 optimizes stratum sample sizes. These overlaps make threshold clipping, knapsack certification, or an LP implementation insufficient by themselves to support the intended contribution. The separate exploratory LP work is outside the nonzero theorem's scope.
