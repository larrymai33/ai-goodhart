# Scope of sharp rank-family audit costs

The v0.6 theorem has a full-prefix hypothesis. Independent calculations in this continuation suggest that its sharp cost limit holds for every nonempty declared best-of-N grid, including grids without budget 1. This is a structural question: a separate monotone-target counterexample shows that small item masses alone cannot justify the envelope cost. Both results concern the original unrestricted zero-table comparator, not power on general safe tables.

Proposed result: with N=max K, L=log(1/delta), and Z=sum_r max_(n in K) w_n(r), OPT_0 ~ C_NEG ~ L*Z/tau uniformly as tau tends to zero and L*N/(P*m*tau) tends to zero, for 0<delta<=1/4. No missing targets are added. The full-prefix coefficient log(N)/e is not asserted for arbitrary sparse K.

Proof mechanism: a four-separated declared skeleton has at most 4Z members. The exact tail identity controls mass below a fixed fraction of each representative's peak. Value windows cover all but a small fraction of envelope mass. Only a uniformly bounded number of geometric envelope bins meet those windows. Apply the previously proved finite hypergeometric lower bound to retained bins. All zero-support, tie, jump, singleton-grid, n=m, and strict unsafe-support endpoints must be covered.

Boundary: monotone normalized target rows sharing a flat head and small tails on exponentially separated prefix lengths can have an unbounded NEGEXP/OPT ratio despite tau and maximum-item density tending to zero. Include a uniform baseline. A conjunction of one head audit and one whole-universe audit gives the comparator upper bound for every fixed fractional table. This is a constructed theoretical boundary, not a model experiment.
